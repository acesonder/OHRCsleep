Know Your Rights

Under the Ontario Human Rights Code, you are protected from discrimination. Addiction and mental health challenges are recognized disabilities.

Your Right to Housing

You cannot be refused housing because of addiction, mental health, or being homeless before.

Learn More

The Ontario Human Rights Commission has stated that municipalities must ensure people have legal places to exist and rest. Blanket bans on sleeping in public may violate your rights if no adequate shelter exists.

Disability Accommodation

Addiction is treated as a disability under the Human Rights Code. Providers must accommodate you.

Learn More

Service providers have a duty to accommodate your disability-related needs up to the point of undue hardship. This includes shelters, social services, and housing providers.

If You're Told to Move

Know what to do when by-law officers or police ask you to leave a public space.

Learn More
  • You have the right to know why you're being asked to move
  • Ask for their name and badge number
  • Document the time and location
  • Contact legal aid if your rights were violated

Filing a Complaint

If you experience discrimination, you can file a complaint with the Human Rights Tribunal.

Learn More

You have one year from the date of discrimination to file an application. Free legal assistance is available through the Human Rights Legal Support Centre at 1-866-625-5179.

If you are refused shelter

  1. Ask for the reason in writing
  2. Record names of staff and date/time
  3. Contact an advocate or legal aid
  4. You may have grounds for a human rights complaint

If enforcement wakes you

  1. Stay calm and ask what by-law you're breaking
  2. Ask if there's available shelter space
  3. Get the officer's name and badge number
  4. Document everything for potential complaints
