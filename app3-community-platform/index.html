Know Your Rights

Under the Ontario Human Rights Code, you are protected. Addiction and mental health are recognized disabilities.

🏠

Right to Housing

You cannot be refused housing because of addiction, mental health, or past homelessness.

Learn More

The Ontario Human Rights Commission has stated that municipalities must ensure people have legal places to exist and rest. Blanket bans on sleeping in public may violate your rights if no adequate shelter exists.

♿

Accommodation

Addiction is a disability. Services must accommodate your needs.

Learn More

Service providers have a duty to accommodate your disability-related needs up to the point of undue hardship. This includes shelters, social services, and housing providers.

🚫

If Told to Move

Know what to do when enforcement asks you to leave.

Learn More
  • Ask why you're being asked to move
  • Get their name and badge number
  • Document time and location
  • Contact legal aid if rights violated
📋

File a Complaint

If discriminated against, file with the Human Rights Tribunal.

Learn More

You have one year from the date of discrimination to file. Free legal help: Human Rights Legal Support Centre at 1-866-625-5179.

🚨 If Refused Shelter

  1. Ask for reason in writing
  2. Record names and date/time
  3. Contact an advocate
  4. May have grounds for complaint

🌙 If Woken by Enforcement

  1. Stay calm, ask what bylaw
  2. Ask if shelter space available
  3. Get officer's name/badge
  4. Document for potential complaint

Take Action

Advocate for temporary zoning changes and housing solutions in Northumberland County.

What We're Asking For

Urgent

1. Emergency Temporary Use Bylaw

Designate 2-3 sites for legal overnight sleeping with basic facilities (washrooms, water, garbage).

Urgent

2. Sleep Deprivation Moratorium

Pause nighttime displacement by bylaw/police. Align with OHRC guidance.

Short-term

3. Tiny Shelter Village Pilot

20-30 small units on municipal land with wraparound supports. Model: Kitchener's A Better Tent City.
